\begin{abstract}
This document specifies the mathematics, protocols, encodings, parameter
validation, and security invariants implemented by \textbf{z0p} version
0.2.1. z0p is a from-scratch, zero-dependency zero-knowledge proving stack in
safe Rust: binary fields, the BabyBear prime field, a quartic extension field,
BLAKE3-256, Merkle trees, a parallel NTT, FRI, sumcheck, univariate and
multilinear polynomial commitments, LogUp lookups, STARK-style AIR systems,
hash-folded IVC, a register-machine AIR, and zero-knowledge masking.
Normative requirements are stated explicitly. Conjectured bit-levels are
marked as such. The production recipe targets a conjectured 100-bit
query/proof-of-work schedule (rate $1/16$, 40 queries, 20-bit grinding); it is
not an end-to-end soundness theorem. The artifact is unaudited research
software.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conventions and notation}

\paragraph{Normative language.} \textbf{MUST}, \textbf{MUST NOT},
\textbf{SHOULD}, and \textbf{MAY} are normative. A proof object is the Rust
value; its encoding is the canonical byte string produced by
\texttt{to\_bytes}. A verifier MUST reject on any failed check with
\texttt{Err} and MUST NOT panic on untrusted input. Public \texttt{Params}
structs have public fields; every entry point MUST re-validate them. Integer
encodings are little-endian. \texttt{usize} is the platform word.

\section{Fields}

\subsection{BabyBear}

\[
p=2^{31}-2^{27}+1=2013265921,\qquad p-1=2^{27}\cdot15,\qquad g=31.
\]
Elements are canonical integers in $[0,p)$. The private representation is a
\texttt{u32} with invariant \texttt{value < p}. The public constructor reduces
modulo $p$; the decoder rejects non-canonical bytes.

\paragraph{Addition and subtraction.} For $a,b<p$:
\[
a+b=\begin{cases}a+b,&a+b<p,\\a+b-p,&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}
\qquad
a-b=\begin{cases}a-b,&a\ge b,\\a-b+p,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
\]
Both fit in \texttt{u32} because $2p<2^{32}$. Negation maps $0\mapsto0$ and
$a\mapsto p-a$.

\paragraph{Multiplication.} Let
\[
\mathrm{MU}=\lfloor2^{64}/p\rfloor=9162596893,\quad
x=ab<p^2<2^{62},\quad
q=((x\gg32)\cdot\mathrm{MU})\gg32.
\]
Then $q\le\lfloor x/p\rfloor$ and $r=x-qp<2p$; a short loop subtracts $p$
until $r<p$. This Barrett reduction is cross-checked against wide \texttt{\%}
on 100,000 random pairs and edge values.

\paragraph{Inversion and halving.} Inversion is Fermat
$a^{-1}=a^{p-2}$, with $0^{-1}=0$ by convention. \texttt{halve(a)} computes
$(a+(a\bmod2)p)/2$, exact because $p$ is odd. Batch inversion uses
Montgomery's trick: one field inversion and $3n$ multiplications; zeros map to
zero and do not contribute to the running product.

\paragraph{Roots of unity.} For $k\le27$,
$\texttt{two\_adic\_generator}(k)=g^{(p-1)/2^k}$ generates the unique
subgroup of order $2^k$. \texttt{primitive\_root}(m) requires a power of two
$m\le2^{27}$.

\subsection{Quartic extension}

Define
\[
\yFpTwo=\yFp[u]/(u^2-11),\qquad
\yFpFour=\yFpTwo[v]/(v^2-u).
\]
An element is $a+bu+cv+duv$, stored as four canonical $\yFp$ coordinates.

\paragraph{Multiplication.} Write $x=A+Cv$, $y=B+Dv$ with
$A,B,C,D\in\yFpTwo$. Then
\[
xy=(AB+uCD)+(AD+CB)v,
\]
using Karatsuba for the cross term
$\mathrm{cross}=(A+C)(B+D)-AB-CD$. $\yFpTwo$ multiplication is
\[
(a_0+a_1u)(b_0+b_1u)
=(a_0b_0+11a_1b_1)+(a_0b_1+a_1b_0)u.
\]

\paragraph{Inversion.} For $x=c+dv$ with $c,d\in\yFpTwo$,
\[
x^{-1}=(c-dv)/(c^2-ud^2).
\]
The denominator inverse uses the $\yFpTwo$ norm
\[
(c_0+c_1u)^{-1}=(c_0-c_1u)/(c_0^2-11c_1^2).
\]
One $\yFpTwo$ inversion therefore suffices. \texttt{inv(0)=0}; no zero branch
is used in the field core.

\paragraph{Canonical encoding.} Each coordinate is four canonical
little-endian bytes; an element is 16 bytes. The decoder checks every
coordinate against $p$.

\texttt{F128} modulus.

\section{Domains, NTT, and Reed--Solomon encoding}

\paragraph{Domains.} The base domain is
\[
H=\{1,\omega,\dots,\omega^{N-1}\},\qquad \omega=\texttt{primitive\_root}(N).
\]
The evaluation domain is the coset
\[
D=\gamma\cdot\langle\Omega\rangle,\qquad |D|=M=N\beta,
\]
with $\Omega=\texttt{primitive\_root}(M)$ and $\gamma=31$. The ceiling is
$M\le2^{27}$. The vanishing polynomial of $H$ is $Z_H(x)=x^N-1$.

\paragraph{Bit reversal.} For $n=2^k$, index $i$ maps to
\[
\mathrm{rev}_k(i)=\mathrm{reverse\_bits}(i)\gg(\texttt{usize::BITS}-k).
\]
Small arrays are permuted in place; large arrays gather into a fresh buffer in
parallel. FRI layers are bit-reversed so each adjacent quad is
$(x,-x,j_0x,-j_0x)$.

\paragraph{NTT.} The forward transform evaluates coefficients at powers of
$\omega$; the inverse interpolates and scales by $1/n$. Cooley--Tukey stages:
\[
\begin{aligned}
u&=\mathrm{block}[j],\\
v&=\mathrm{block}[j+\ell/2]\cdot\mathrm{twiddle}[j\cdot(n/\ell)],\\
\mathrm{block}[j]&=u+v,\\
\mathrm{block}[j+\ell/2]&=u-v.
\end{aligned}
\]
Twiddles are chained powers $1,\mathrm{root},\dots,\mathrm{root}^{n/2-1}$.
The inverse folds $1/n$ into the final stage. Domains must be nonempty powers
of two at most $2^{27}$.

\paragraph{Coset evaluation.} For coefficients $c_0,\dots,c_{n-1}$ and blowup
$\beta$, compute $M=n\beta$ values $c(\gamma\Omega^i)$ by multiplying $c_i$
by $\gamma^i$, zero-padding to $M$, and applying the forward NTT. Extension
coset evaluation applies the base transform to each of the four coordinates
and recombines.

\section{BLAKE3 and Merkle commitments}

\paragraph{BLAKE3-256.} Unkeyed BLAKE3 with 256-bit output: 16-word state,
7 ARX rounds, message permutation
$[2,6,3,10,7,0,4,13,1,11,12,5,9,14,15,8]$, flags
\texttt{CHUNK\_START=1}, \texttt{CHUNK\_END=2}, \texttt{PARENT=4},
\texttt{ROOT=8}, 1024-byte chunks, 64-byte blocks, and Bao-style
merge-while-even stacking. Validated against reference vectors for empty
input, \texttt{abc}, one chunk, one byte over a chunk, two chunks, and
multi-chunk inputs crossing boundaries. Incremental updates with arbitrary
splits equal the one-shot digest.

\paragraph{Merkle trees.} A complete binary tree over leaves padded by
duplicating the last leaf to a power of two. For raw leaves:
\[
\mathrm{leaf}(d)=H(0x00\|d),\qquad
\mathrm{node}(l,r)=H(0x01\|l\|r).
\]
FRI quad trees and AIR trace trees use 64-byte leaves hashed with standard
BLAKE3 (one compression per full block); internal nodes use the same
\texttt{0x01} construction.

A path is a sequence $(\mathrm{sibling},\mathrm{is\_left})$. Verification
recomputes the leaf digest, folds upward, and compares to the root. A path is
valid for index $i$ only if for every level $k$,
\[
\mathrm{is\_left}_k=((i\gg k)\&1=0).
\]
This index binding is mandatory. Without it, a valid opening for leaf $j$
would verify at any other in-range index with the same path length.

\paragraph{Caps.} A cap at drop $d$ is
\[
\mathrm{cap}(d)=\mathrm{layers}[\mathrm{depth}-\min(d,\mathrm{depth})],
\]
containing $2^{\min(d,\mathrm{depth})}$ digests. Openings omit the top $d$
path steps and authenticate the remaining path to the selected cap entry.
Cap height is capped at 8 in every public \texttt{Params};
\texttt{MAX\_CAP\_DIGESTS}=256.

For FRI, opened layers commit one 64-byte leaf per quad; the final layer is
sent in the clear and its cap is recomputed from \texttt{final\_poly}. For
AIR, a 64-byte trace leaf contains eight joint rows ($A\|B$, 8 bytes per
row). The verifier checks the row at $(\mathrm{index}\&7)\cdot8$ and
authenticates the whole leaf at $\mathrm{index}/8$.

\section{Fiat--Shamir transcript and proof of work}

\paragraph{Framing.} \texttt{absorb(label,data)} appends
\[
\texttt{u64\_le(label.len())}\|\text{label}\|
\texttt{u64\_le(data.len())}\|\text{data}.
\]
The implementation stores the history as a streaming BLAKE3 state. A squeeze
clones the state, appends
\[
\texttt{u64\_le(label.len())}\|\text{label}\|\texttt{u64\_le(counter)},
\]
finalizes, increments the counter, and absorbs the 32-byte output back into
the history. This is byte-identical to hashing the full concatenation but
independent of history length.

\paragraph{Challenges.} \texttt{challenge\_f64} uses the first 8 bytes;
\texttt{challenge\_f128} uses 16 bytes; \texttt{challenge\_bb} reduces an
8-byte word modulo $p$ (bias below $2^{-31}$); \texttt{challenge\_ext} reduces
four 4-byte coordinates; \texttt{challenge\_index} masks low bits for a
nonzero power-of-two domain and returns \texttt{None} otherwise.

\paragraph{Proof of work.} \texttt{grind(label,d)} computes
\[
\mathrm{head}=H(\mathrm{history})\|\texttt{u64\_le(label.len())}\|\mathrm{label}
\]
and searches for the smallest nonce with at least $d$ leading zero bits under
$H(\mathrm{head}\|\texttt{u64\_le(nonce)})$. The banded parallel search
returns the same nonce as a sequential scan. Protocol parameters cap $d\le32$;
\texttt{grind} fails closed above 32 by absorbing nonce $0$ and returning
$0$. \texttt{verify\_grind} re-hashes and accepts only if the nonce meets the
target; it hard-rejects $d>256$. Queries are sampled after the nonce is
absorbed.

\section{FRI polynomial commitment}

\subsection{Parameters and commitment}

A FRI parameter set is
\[
(\beta,Q,F,g,h),\qquad
\beta\ge2,\quad Q\ge1,\quad 2\le F\le1024,\quad g\le32,\quad h\le8,
\]
with $F<M$ and $M/F$ a power of four. \texttt{commit} maps $n$ coefficients
to $M=n\beta$ coset evaluations with a shifted NTT. \texttt{commit\_evals}
accepts already-expanded LDE values with a declared degree bound $d<M$. Both
paths validate parameters before the encoding NTT, reject empty or
non-power-of-two lengths, enforce $M\le2^{27}$, and bind $(M,n,d)$ under
\texttt{fri-dims} before any challenge. The rate gate
\[
d+1\le M/2
\]
is enforced at prove and verify.

\subsection{Arity-4 folding}

Layers are bit-reversed. Each adjacent quad sits at
$(x,-x,j_0x,-j_0x)$ for the fixed fourth root $j_0$, sharing $x^4$. Writing
$P(X)=A+BX+CX^2+DX^3$ in $Y=X^4$, the fold is
\[
\yFold(e_0,e_1,e_2,e_3)=A+r_0B+r_1C+r_2D,
\]
with
\[
\begin{aligned}
A&=(e_0+e_1+e_2+e_3)/4,\\
B&=((e_0-e_1)-j_0(e_2-e_3))/(4x),\\
C&=((e_0+e_1)-(e_2+e_3))/(4x^2),\\
D&=((e_0-e_1)+j_0(e_2-e_3))/(4x^3),
\end{aligned}
\]
and $(r_0,r_1,r_2)\in\yFpFour^3$. Point inverses are base-field values and
are batch-inverted once per layer. Each fold quarters the degree.

\subsection{Proof and verification}

A proof carries the final polynomial in the clear, one opening per query
(four $\yFpFour$ values per folded quad plus the middle Merkle path segment),
and a PoW nonce. Verification performs:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.3em]
\item validate dimensions, cap shapes, fold count, and the rate gate;
\item absorb dimensions, cap digests, and the final polynomial;
\item check the PoW nonce against the replayed history;
\item verify the final layer by divided differences against residual
$d\gg2f$ at the true folded domain points;
\item recompute the final layer cap from \texttt{final\_poly} and compare it
to the commitment;
\item sample query indices after grinding and check every Merkle path and fold
relation, chaining intermediate parents forward and the last parent into
\texttt{final\_poly}.
\end{enumerate}

The final degree check is non-vacuous: a one-point final layer would let any
value interpolate a degree-0 polynomial, so $F\ge2$ is mandatory and
\[
(d\gg2f)+1<F
\]
is enforced. A fold-free instance ($F=M$) is rejected because it has no query
openings binding \texttt{final\_poly} to the commitment.

\subsection{Conjectured soundness}

Under the Johnson-radius heuristic, a word at distance $\delta$ from the code
survives one query with probability at most $1-\delta$, where
$\delta\approx1-\sqrt{\rho}$.

\section{Multilinear polynomials and sumcheck}

A multilinear polynomial with $n$ variables stores $2^n$ evaluations in
MSB-first order. \texttt{evaluate} folds the least-significant variable first.
The equality polynomial is
\[
\yEq(r,x)=\prod_i\bigl(r_ix_i+(1-r_i)(1-x_i)\bigr).
\]
A summand is a sum of terms
$f(x)=\sum_kc_k\prod_{j\in\mathrm{factors}_k}\mathrm{mle}_j(x)$ with
$c_k\in\yFpFour$. Sumcheck proves $S=\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^n}f(x)$ by sending
round polynomials of degree at most the maximum factor count. Verification
checks $g_i(0)+g_i(1)=g_{i-1}(r_{i-1})$, samples $r_i\in\yFpFour$, and
finally checks the oracle value. If the oracle is committed, each final value
is opened at the challenge point.

\section{Univariate, Gemini, and BaseFold commitments}

\subsection{UPCs}

\texttt{upcs} commits a univariate polynomial as a Merkle-committed
Reed--Solomon LDE. To prove $g(\beta)=y$, the prover commits
$q=(g-y)/(X-\beta)$ and proves both $g$ and $q$ low-degree with FRI; the
verifier checks $(X-\beta)q=g-y$ at the quotient's query points. Multi-point
openings use $I$ interpolating the claims and $Z=\prod_j(X-p_j)$, with
$q=(g-I)/Z$ and $g=I+Zq$. When the points determine the polynomial
($k=\mathrm{poly\_len}$), the quotient is zero and the verifier checks $g$
against the interpolant directly. Optional Merkle caps shorten paths and are
authenticated against the commitment root.

\subsection{Gemini}

Gemini commits the monomial-coefficient encoding
$g_0(X)=\sum_bc_bX^{\mathrm{index}(b)}$ and proves $f(r)=y$ by folding one
variable at a time:
\[
g_{i-1}(X)=A_i(X^2)+XB_i(X^2),\qquad
g_i(Y)=A_i(Y)+r_iB_i(Y).
\]
Each committed layer is opened once with a multi-point UPCs proof at all
points its relations use. Layer commitments are absorbed before the fold
betas. Proof size is linear, not constant. Each fold identity has degree below
$2^{n-i+1}$, so one random check misses a nonzero identity with probability at
most $2^{n-i+1}/p^4$.

\subsection{BaseFold prototype}

BaseFold commits the coefficient encoding with FRI and reduces $f(r)=y$ to a
coefficient-oracle evaluation $c(\rho)$ with sumcheck. The prover folds the
LDE with the M\"obius/eq rule
\[
(1-\rho_i)E(X)+\rho_iO(X)
\]
rather than the coefficient-encoding rule $E(X)+\rho_iO(X)$, so the final FRI
constant is exactly $c(\rho)$. The verifier recomputes
\[
w(\rho)=\prod_i(1-\rho_i+\rho_ir_i)
\]
and checks $f(r)=c(\rho)w(\rho)$. Proofs carry one root, $n-1$ layer roots,
sumcheck rounds, and one pair plus one Merkle path per layer per query.
Optional caps store a digest frontier per layer and hash it back to the layer
root. Batched multi-point openings use the multilinear basis table and are
capped at 1024 points. The prototype is not wired into the AIR/STARK stack
and has no end-to-end soundness proof; it backs the committed lookup.

\section{Lookup arguments}

\subsection{LogUp in clear}

For witness $w$ and table $t$ padded to powers of two, the prover computes
first-index multiplicities $m$, samples $\beta$ avoiding poles, and forms
\[
q^w_i=\frac{1}{\beta+w_i},\qquad
q^t_j=\frac{m_j}{\beta+t_j}.
\]
Four sumchecks establish the rational identity
$\sum_iq^w_i=\sum_jq^t_j$ and the read-checks
$q^w_i(\beta+w_i)=1$, $q^t_j(\beta+t_j)=m_j$. The verifier has witness and
table in clear and recomputes canonical multiplicities exactly; fractional
multiplicities are rejected. The API is sound for membership but not hiding.

\subsection{Committed LogUp}

\texttt{lookup\_pcs} commits witness, table, multiplicities, and both quotient
columns with BaseFold. The verifier sees five commitments and eight openings.
The read-checks use the committed columns directly:
\[
\yEq\cdot\bigl(q\cdot(\beta+v)-\mathrm{target}\bigr)=0,
\]
expanded as $\yEq qv+\beta\yEq q-\yEq\,\mathrm{target}$. A fresh $\gamma$
folds the total multiplicity weight into the sum-t claim:
\[
\sum(q^t+\gamma m)=\sum q^w+\gamma W.
\]
The verifier rejects $\gamma=0$. Every final sumcheck value is opened against
its commitment. At 1,024-in-1,024 with $B16Q40G20C4$, the prototype measures
273.01~ms prove, 5.77~ms verify, and 642,443 bytes. It is committed-input but
not zero-knowledge and does not constrain columns to the base field.

\section{AIR systems}

\subsection{Fibonacci AIR}

Trace $A[0]=a_0$, $B[0]=b_0$, $A[i+1]=B[i]$, $B[i+1]=A[i]+B[i]$, claim
$B[N-1]=b_{\mathrm{last}}$. With $w_{\mathrm{last}}=\omega^{N-1}$ and
$Z_{\mathrm{trans}}(x)=(x^N-1)/(x-w_{\mathrm{last}})$:
\[
\begin{aligned}
C(x)={}&\alpha_0\frac{a'-b}{Z_{\mathrm{trans}}(x)}
+\alpha_1\frac{b'-(a+b)}{Z_{\mathrm{trans}}(x)}\\
&+\alpha_2\frac{a-a_0}{x-1}
+\alpha_3\frac{b-b_0}{x-1}
+\alpha_4\frac{b-b_{\mathrm{last}}}{x-w_{\mathrm{last}}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Five \texttt{fib/alpha} challenges; $\deg C<N$ iff the trace is valid.
Dividing, not multiplying, by $Z_{\mathrm{trans}}$ is load-bearing. Blowup is
at least 4. Next-row values are the same committed columns at the
$\omega$-shifted LDE index, authenticated by a second Merkle path.

\subsection{Range check AIR}

Value column $V$ plus $n$ bit columns ($1\le n\le24$), with challenges
$\alpha_{\mathrm{decomp}}$, $\{\alpha_j\}$, $\alpha_{\mathrm{bound}}$:
\[
C=\alpha_{\mathrm{decomp}}\frac{V-\sum_j2^jC_j}{Z_H}
+\sum_j\alpha_j\frac{C_j(C_j-1)}{Z_H}
+\alpha_{\mathrm{bound}}\frac{V-\mathrm{value}}{x-1}.
\]
Booleanity doubles the masked degree; masked callers should size blowup and
queries for a rate near $2/\beta$. The verifier caps the declared bound at the
largest honest masked degree.

\subsection{Table membership AIR}

\[
C(x)=\alpha_{\mathrm{member}}\frac{\prod_{t\in T}(v-t)}{Z_H(x)}
+\alpha_{\mathrm{bound}}\frac{v-\mathrm{value}}{x-1}.
\]
The honest composition bound is $|T|(n+k-1)-n$ for $n$ rows and $k$ maskers.
The tiny-table \texttt{for\_security} tuner floors blowup at 4 so FRI always
has a valid final layer.

\subsection{Hash-folded IVC}

Segments of \texttt{seg\_steps} Fibonacci rows are committed independently
with explicit in/out claims. The verifier checks chain links, then verifies
one shared FRI proof over
\[
V=\sum_s\gamma_sC_s
\]
with per-step challenges. A far-from-code segment keeps the combination far
with overwhelming probability over the gammas. The statement is existential
over the number of segments; callers requiring an exact step count must check
\texttt{proof.steps.len()}. The accumulator retains each segment's LDE,
Merkle tree, and composition until finalization.

\subsection{Register-machine execution}

A public two-register straight-line program runs from a private initial state
to a public output. Opcodes are \texttt{Add}, \texttt{Mul}, \texttt{Swap},
\texttt{SetA}, \texttt{SetB}, and \texttt{Halt}; the last row is the public
output and has no outgoing transition. Public one-hot selectors and immediate
polynomials drive transition numerators $T,U$:
\[
\begin{aligned}
C(x)={}&\alpha_0\frac{T(x)}{Z_{\mathrm{trans}}(x)}
+\alpha_1\frac{U(x)}{Z_{\mathrm{trans}}(x)}\\
&+\alpha_2\frac{A(x)-a_{\mathrm{last}}}{x-w_{\mathrm{last}}}
+\alpha_3\frac{B(x)-b_{\mathrm{last}}}{x-w_{\mathrm{last}}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Plain composition degree is below $2n-1$, so blowup is at least 8. Masked
columns have degree $\mathrm{padded}-1$; the \texttt{Mul} transition is
quadratic, so the prover declares $2(n+n_{\mathrm{rand}}-1)$ and the verifier
accepts up to $2\cdot\mathrm{padded}-2$. Each query reveals the current and
shifted rows, so the mask budget is $2Q$. The program and output remain
public; there is no control flow or memory.

\subsection{Recursion fragment}

\texttt{recursion} proves one FRI query's fold chain as a small STARK. Public
inputs are the outer \texttt{lde\_len}, leaf, per-depth challenges, the
layer-0 quad, and the output; the witness is the sequence of quads and folded
outputs. Constraints cover the fold identity, the selected next-layer element,
the initial quad, and the final output. The fragment does not yet verify
BLAKE3 paths, the final degree check, or transcript derivation inside the AIR.

\section{Zero-knowledge masking}

Masked proving maps a column $T$ of degree $<n$ to
\[
M(x)=T(x)+Z_H(x)R(x),
\]
where $R$ has $n_{\mathrm{rand}}$ uniform coefficients. $Z_H$ vanishes on
every base row, so trace values and AIR quotient identities are preserved.
Off-domain, the mask term is nonzero. Evaluations of $R$ at $k$ distinct
points are jointly uniform when $n_{\mathrm{rand}}\ge k$ (Vandermonde),
giving perfect hiding of query responses.

remain visible. \texttt{lookup}, \texttt{sumcheck}, \texttt{upcs},
\texttt{gemini}, and \texttt{basefold} are not hiding.

\section{Parameter validation and sizing}

Every entry point validates:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.3em]
\item domains are nonempty powers of two within $2^{27}$;
\item blowup is a power of two ($\ge4$ for AIR/fold, $\ge2$ for
FRI/range/table, $\ge8$ for Gemini/VM);
\item queries $\ge1$ and $\le2^{20}$;
\item \texttt{final\_size} $\ge2$, power of two, $\le1024$, and strictly
below the LDE size;
\item $M/F$ is a power of four;
\item cap height $\le8$;
\item grinding bits $\le32$;
\item rate gate $d+1\le M/2$;
\item residual bound non-vacuous: $(d\gg2f)+1<F$;
\item recursion outer \texttt{lde\_len} $\le2^{27}$.
\end{itemize}

\texttt{Params::for\_security(len,\dots,target)} selects blowup 16, computes
millibits per query from the rate and composition degree, splits the target
between queries and grinding, and chooses \texttt{final\_size} and
\texttt{cap\_height} with \texttt{tune\_shape}. For target 100, the
AIR/FRI production shape is rate $1/16$, 40 queries, and 20-bit grinding.
The FRI tuner never selects a one-point final layer and caps normal finals at
32; the hard verifier cap is 1024.

\section{Security analysis}

\paragraph{Assumptions.} BLAKE3 collision/preimage resistance; FRI soundness
under the Johnson-radius heuristic; Fiat--Shamir in the (quantum)
random-oracle model; standard Reed--Solomon list-decoding bounds. No
discrete-log, pairing, or setup assumptions.

\paragraph{Adversary model.} The adversary controls proof bytes, commitments,
public parameters, and public statements. It may choose malformed counts,
paths, caps, field encodings, and dimensions. The verifier must fail closed,
never panic, and never allocate based on an unchecked count. The adversary may
also choose the prover's witness and strategy; soundness is argued against a
malicious prover, not only random errors.

\paragraph{Enforced invariants.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.3em]
\item Rate gate at prove and verify.
\item Verifier-derived domains and query indices.
\item Non-vacuous final divided-difference check.
\item Cap/path shape pins before hashing.
\item Direction bits bound to claimed indices.
\item \texttt{fri-dims} dimension binding.
\item Canonical-only decoding with hard caps.
\item PoW bound to the full history; queries sampled after grinding.
\item Committed read-checks in \texttt{lookup\_pcs}.
\item Correct masked composition bounds in VM.
\item Next-row openings authenticated against the same trace cap.
\item No allocation before count limits are checked.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Closed findings.} Internal adversarial review has closed Merkle
path index substitution across all verifiers and hand-rolled FRI/BaseFold
walks; one-point FRI final layers and fold-free FRI; final cap not bound to
\texttt{final\_poly}; \texttt{lookup\_pcs} read-checks using uncommitted
shifted MLEs; VM masked \texttt{Mul} degree under-declaration; AIR/VM ZK mask
budgets undercounting revealed values; batched BaseFold openings using the
equality table instead of the multilinear basis table; and prover allocation
ordering, cap-height/roundtrip limits, recursion domain bounds, checked
buffer arithmetic, and transcript/grinding hardening.

\paragraph{Residual risk.} The system is unaudited. The query/PoW figures are
conjectured. The small-table AIR scales with table size. \texttt{lookup\_pcs}
is not zero-knowledge and does not constrain columns to the base field.
BaseFold, Gemini, VM, and recursion are prototypes. Side channels in the host
OS, compiler, or CPU are out of scope. Do not deploy for high-value statements
before an external audit.
