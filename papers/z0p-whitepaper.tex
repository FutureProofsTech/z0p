\begin{abstract}
We present \textbf{z0p}, a complete zero-knowledge proving stack implemented
from first principles in safe Rust with no dependencies beyond the compiler
and the operating system. Binary fields, the BabyBear prime field, a quartic
extension field, BLAKE3-256, Merkle trees, a parallel NTT, FRI, sumcheck,
univariate and multilinear polynomial commitments, LogUp lookups, four
STARK-style AIR systems, hash-folded IVC, a minimal register-machine AIR, and
zero-knowledge masking are owned by one crate under
\texttt{\#![forbid(unsafe\_code)]}. The construction is transparent: no
trusted setup, no elliptic curves, no pairings. Security reduces to
BLAKE3-256, Reed--Solomon proximity testing, and Fiat--Shamir.

Production parameters target a \emph{conjectured} 100-bit
query/proof-of-work schedule: rate $1/16$, 40 queries, and 20-bit grinding.
Algebraic challenges are drawn from the quartic extension
$\wFpFour=\wFpTwo[v]/(v^2-u)$, $\wFpTwo=\wFp[u]/(u^2-11)$, giving
approximately 124 bits of per-challenge algebraic entropy. On a 16-core
reference machine, release profile, \texttt{rustc}~1.99.0, a 1,048,576-step
Fibonacci AIR proves in 1.73355~s (1.653~$\mu$s/step, 604,872 steps/s),
verifies in 1.13~ms, and produces a 203,553-byte (198.8~KiB) proof.
Verification is essentially independent of trace size: FRI domain points are
derived on demand rather than materialized.

The artifact is validated by 277 library tests, 13 security tests with 479
hostile attempts, 10 red-team tests with 37 targeted attacks plus a
500-input garbage storm, 11 hardening tests including exhaustive per-bit AIR
decoder fuzzing and 14,000 random mutations across seven encodings, and 5
documentation tests. Every measured forgery is rejected; hostile input fails
closed with \texttt{Err}, never a panic. The system is explicitly
\emph{unaudited}: the contribution is an auditable engineering artifact and a
reproducible evidence package, not a claim of production-grade end-to-end
soundness.
\end{abstract}

\section{The problem}

Practical zero-knowledge infrastructure asks its users to trust a large
surface: pairing-friendly curves whose security assumptions are contested,
multi-party setup ceremonies whose toxic waste must be destroyed,
cryptographic libraries stacked dozens of dependencies deep, and protocol
behavior described in papers and slide decks but not reproducible from the
code that runs.

At the same time, large-scale quantum computing threatens every deployed
pairing- and discrete-log-based proof system. The systems that survive that
transition will be hash-based, transparently set up, small enough to audit,
and tested hard enough to believe. z0p is an experiment in how small that
trusted base can be made while retaining a useful proof toolkit.

\section{Design goals and non-goals}

\paragraph{Goals.} (1) From zero: every cryptographic layer is implemented in
the crate; \texttt{cargo tree} is one node. (2) Transparent and plausibly
post-quantum: BLAKE3 and Reed--Solomon proximity testing; no curves, pairings,
or setup. (3) Safe by construction: \texttt{\#![forbid(unsafe\_code)]},
fallible public APIs, canonical fail-closed decoding, hostile parameters
re-validated at every entry point. (4) Quantified security: explicit query and
proof-of-work budgets. (5) Evidence over claims: adversarial suites,
byte-exact proof-size assertions, and a pinned benchmark regression gate.
(6) Auditability: one crate, one wire format, one formal specification, and a
documented threat model.

\paragraph{Non-goals.} z0p is not an audited production system, a general
circuit compiler, a zkVM, a private-table lookup protocol, or a
side-channel-hardened hardware token. \texttt{lookup} and \texttt{sumcheck}
expose tables in clear; \texttt{lookup\_pcs} hides columns from the wire but
is not zero-knowledge; \texttt{basefold}, \texttt{gemini},
\texttt{lookup\_pcs}, \texttt{vm}, and \texttt{recursion} are prototypes.
The approximately 100-bit figure is a query/PoW sizing, not a theorem.

\section{Threat model and trust base}

\paragraph{Adversary-facing surface.} The verifier is the adversary-facing
surface. Untrusted inputs are proof and commitment bytes, public parameters
(including hand-built \texttt{Params} structs whose fields are public), and
public statements. The verifier must fail closed with \texttt{Err}, never
panic, and never perform unbounded allocation or work. It must derive all
challenge-dependent data from the transcript and bind every public input
before sampling challenges.

\paragraph{Trust base.} The Rust compiler and standard library; BLAKE3-256 as
a collision-resistant hash; the Reed--Solomon/FRI proximity heuristic and
Fiat--Shamir in the (quantum) random-oracle model; the correctness of the
field and NTT implementations, cross-tested against independent
Horner/\texttt{\%} references. No trusted setup, elliptic-curve group,
pairing, or discrete-log assumption is used. Shor's algorithm does not apply
to the cryptographic core.

\paragraph{Out of scope.} Host OS, compiler, CPU microarchitecture,
power/EM side channels, physical attacks, denial of service by a prover
against itself, and high-value production deployment before an external
audit.

\section{Cryptographic foundations}

\paragraph{Fields.} Program words use binary fields $GF(2^8)$,
$GF(2^{64})$, $GF(2^{128})$. Polynomial domains use BabyBear
\[
p=2^{31}-2^{27}+1=2013265921,\qquad p-1=2^{27}\cdot15,
\]
with generator $31$. Multiplicative subgroups of order $2^k$ exist for
$k\le27$. Elements serialize as four canonical little-endian bytes;
multiplication uses Barrett reduction with
$\mu=\lfloor2^{64}/p\rfloor$; inversion is Fermat
($a^{p-2}$, mapping zero to zero); batch inversion uses Montgomery's trick.

All algebraic challenges use the tower
\[
\wFpTwo=\wFp[u]/(u^2-11),\qquad
\wFpFour=\wFpTwo[v]/(v^2-u),
\]
represented as $a+bu+cv+duv$ with four canonical coordinates. $11$ is a
quadratic non-residue in $\wFp$, and $u$ is a non-square in $\wFpTwo$.
Inversion uses the tower norm: for $x=c+dv$,
$x^{-1}=(c-dv)/(c^2-ud^2)$, so one $\wFpTwo$ inversion suffices.

\paragraph{Domains and NTT.} The trace domain is
$H=\{1,\omega,\dots,\omega^{N-1}\}$; the evaluation domain is the coset
$D=\gamma\cdot\langle\Omega\rangle$ of size $M=N\beta$, with coset shift
$\gamma=31$ and $M\le2^{27}$. The vanishing polynomial is
$Z_H(x)=x^N-1$. The NTT is a table-driven Cooley--Tukey transform with
bit-reversal, deterministic parallel stages, and inverse scaling folded into
the last stage. Reed--Solomon extension interpolates and evaluates on the
coset.

\paragraph{BLAKE3 and Merkle trees.} Unkeyed BLAKE3-256 is implemented from
the specification: 7-round ARX compression, the standard message permutation,
flags \texttt{CHUNK\_START}, \texttt{CHUNK\_END}, \texttt{PARENT},
\texttt{ROOT}, 1024-byte chunks, and Bao-style merge-while-even stacking.
Column trees use
\[
\mathrm{leaf}(d)=H(0x00\|d),\qquad
\mathrm{node}(l,r)=H(0x01\|l\|r).
\]
FRI layers commit one 64-byte leaf per quad (four 16-byte $\wFpFour$ values);
AIR trace layers commit one 64-byte leaf per eight joint rows. Caps expose an
upper tree layer of $2^h$ digests, and every path direction bit is bound to
the claimed leaf index. Cap height is capped at 8.

\paragraph{Transcript.} The transcript is append-only and length-prefix
framed. Each absorb writes
$(\text{label\_len},\text{label},\text{data\_len},\text{data})$ into a
streaming BLAKE3 state. A challenge clones that state, appends
$(\text{label\_len},\text{label},\text{counter})$, finalizes, and feeds the
output back. This is byte-identical to hashing the full concatenation but
independent of history length. $\wFp$ challenges reduce a 64-bit word modulo
$p$; query indices mask low bits for power-of-two domains; $\wFpFour$
challenges reduce four coordinates.

\paragraph{Proof of work.} \texttt{grind(label,d)} finds the smallest nonce
with at least $d$ leading zero bits under the history commitment, using a
banded parallel search identical to a sequential scan. Protocol parameters
cap grinding at 32 bits; \texttt{grind} fails closed above 32.
\texttt{verify\_grind} checks the nonce and absorbs it only on success.
Queries are sampled after the nonce is absorbed.

\section{FRI polynomial commitment}

A FRI parameter set is $(\beta,Q,F,g,h)$ with $\beta\ge2$, $Q\ge1$,
$2\le F\le1024$, $g\le32$, $h\le8$, $F<M$, and $M/F$ a power of four.
\texttt{commit} maps $n$ coefficients to $M=n\beta$ coset evaluations with a
shifted NTT. \texttt{commit\_evals} accepts already-expanded LDE values with
a declared degree bound $d<M$. Both paths validate parameters before the
encoding NTT, reject empty or non-power-of-two lengths, enforce $M\le2^{27}$,
and bind $(M,n,d)$ under \texttt{fri-dims} before any challenge. The rate gate
$d+1\le M/2$ is enforced at prove and verify.

\paragraph{Arity-4 folding.} Layers are bit-reversed. Each adjacent quad sits
at $(x,-x,j_0x,-j_0x)$ for the fixed fourth root $j_0$, sharing $x^4$.
Writing $P(X)=A+BX+CX^2+DX^3$ in $Y=X^4$, the fold is
\[
\wFold(e_0,e_1,e_2,e_3)=A+r_0B+r_1C+r_2D,
\]
with
\[
\begin{aligned}
A&=(e_0+e_1+e_2+e_3)/4,\\
B&=((e_0-e_1)-j_0(e_2-e_3))/(4x),\\
C&=((e_0+e_1)-(e_2+e_3))/(4x^2),\\
D&=((e_0-e_1)+j_0(e_2-e_3))/(4x^3),
\end{aligned}
\]
and $(r_0,r_1,r_2)\in\wFpFour^3$. Point inverses are base-field values and
are batch-inverted once per layer. Each fold quarters the degree.

\paragraph{Proof and verification.} A proof carries the final polynomial in
the clear, one opening per query (four $\wFpFour$ values per folded quad plus
the middle Merkle path segment), and a PoW nonce. The verifier validates
dimensions, cap shapes, fold count, and the rate gate; absorbs dimensions,
cap digests, and the final polynomial; checks the PoW; verifies the final
layer by divided differences against residual $d\gg2f$; recomputes the final
cap from \texttt{final\_poly}; and checks every query path and fold relation.
A one-point final layer is rejected because it would make the degree check
vacuous; a fold-free instance is rejected because it has no query openings
binding \texttt{final\_poly} to the commitment.

\paragraph{Conjectured soundness.} Under the Johnson-radius heuristic, a word
at distance $\delta$ from the code survives one query with probability at most
$1-\delta$, where $\delta\approx1-\sqrt{\rho}$.

\section{Proof systems and commitments}

\paragraph{Sumcheck.} A multilinear polynomial with $n$ variables stores
$2^n$ evaluations in MSB-first order. The equality polynomial is
\[
\wEq(r,x)=\prod_i\bigl(r_ix_i+(1-r_i)(1-x_i)\bigr).
\]
A summand is a sum of terms
$f(x)=\sum_kc_k\prod_{j\in\mathrm{factors}_k}\mathrm{mle}_j(x)$ with
$c_k\in\wFpFour$. Sumcheck proves $S=\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^n}f(x)$ by sending
round polynomials of degree at most the maximum factor count. Verification
checks $g_i(0)+g_i(1)=g_{i-1}(r_{i-1})$, samples $r_i$, and finally checks
the oracle value.

\paragraph{UPCs.} \texttt{upcs} commits a univariate polynomial as a
Merkle-committed Reed--Solomon LDE. To prove $g(\beta)=y$, the prover commits
$q=(g-y)/(X-\beta)$ and proves both $g$ and $q$ low-degree with FRI; the
verifier checks $(X-\beta)q=g-y$ at the quotient's query points. Multi-point
openings use $I$ interpolating the claims and $Z=\prod_j(X-p_j)$, with
$q=(g-I)/Z$ and $g=I+Zq$. When the points determine the polynomial, the
quotient is zero and the verifier checks $g$ against the interpolant directly.

\paragraph{Gemini.} Gemini commits the monomial-coefficient encoding
$g_0(X)=\sum_bc_bX^{\mathrm{index}(b)}$ and proves $f(r)=y$ by folding one
variable at a time:
\[
g_{i-1}(X)=A_i(X^2)+XB_i(X^2),\qquad
g_i(Y)=A_i(Y)+r_iB_i(Y).
\]
Each committed layer is opened once with a multi-point UPCs proof at all
points its relations use. Layer commitments are absorbed before the fold
betas. Proof size is linear, not constant.

\paragraph{BaseFold prototype.} BaseFold commits the coefficient encoding
with FRI and reduces $f(r)=y$ to a coefficient-oracle evaluation $c(\rho)$
with sumcheck. The prover folds the LDE with the M\"obius/eq rule
$(1-\rho_i)E(X)+\rho_iO(X)$ rather than the coefficient-encoding rule
$E(X)+\rho_iO(X)$, so the final FRI constant is exactly $c(\rho)$. The
verifier recomputes
\[
w(\rho)=\prod_i(1-\rho_i+\rho_ir_i)
\]
and checks $f(r)=c(\rho)w(\rho)$. Proofs carry one root, $n-1$ layer roots,
sumcheck rounds, and one pair plus one Merkle path per layer per query.
Optional caps store a digest frontier per layer and hash it back to the layer
root. Batched multi-point openings use the multilinear basis table and are
capped at 1024 points. The prototype is not wired into the AIR/STARK stack
and has no end-to-end soundness proof; it backs the committed lookup.

\paragraph{LogUp lookups.} For witness $w$ and table $t$ padded to powers of
two, the prover computes first-index multiplicities $m$, samples $\beta$
avoiding poles, and forms
\[
q^w_i=\frac{1}{\beta+w_i},\qquad
q^t_j=\frac{m_j}{\beta+t_j}.
\]
Four sumchecks establish the rational identity
$\sum_iq^w_i=\sum_jq^t_j$ and the read-checks
$q^w_i(\beta+w_i)=1$, $q^t_j(\beta+t_j)=m_j$. The verifier has witness and
table in clear and recomputes canonical multiplicities exactly; fractional
multiplicities are rejected. The API is sound for membership but not hiding.

\texttt{lookup\_pcs} commits witness, table, multiplicities, and both quotient
columns with BaseFold. The verifier sees five commitments and eight openings.
The read-checks use the committed columns directly:
\[
\wEq\cdot\bigl(q\cdot(\beta+v)-\mathrm{target}\bigr)=0,
\]
expanded as $\wEq qv+\beta\wEq q-\wEq\,\mathrm{target}$. A fresh $\gamma$
folds the total multiplicity weight into the sum-t claim:
$\sum(q^t+\gamma m)=\sum q^w+\gamma W$. The verifier rejects $\gamma=0$ and
opens every final sumcheck value against its commitment. At
1,024-in-1,024 with $B16Q40G20C4$, the prototype measures 273.01~ms prove,
5.77~ms verify, and 642,443 bytes. It is committed-input but not
zero-knowledge and does not constrain columns to the base field.

\section{AIR systems}

\paragraph{Fibonacci AIR.} Trace $A[0]=a_0$, $B[0]=b_0$, $A[i+1]=B[i]$,
$B[i+1]=A[i]+B[i]$, claim $B[N-1]=b_{\mathrm{last}}$. With
$w_{\mathrm{last}}=\omega^{N-1}$ and
$Z_{\mathrm{trans}}(x)=(x^N-1)/(x-w_{\mathrm{last}})$:
\[
\begin{aligned}
C(x)={}&\alpha_0\frac{a'-b}{Z_{\mathrm{trans}}(x)}
+\alpha_1\frac{b'-(a+b)}{Z_{\mathrm{trans}}(x)}\\
&+\alpha_2\frac{a-a_0}{x-1}
+\alpha_3\frac{b-b_0}{x-1}
+\alpha_4\frac{b-b_{\mathrm{last}}}{x-w_{\mathrm{last}}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Five \texttt{fib/alpha} challenges; $\deg C<N$ iff the trace is valid.
Dividing, not multiplying, by $Z_{\mathrm{trans}}$ is load-bearing. Blowup is
at least 4. Next-row values are the same committed columns at the
$\omega$-shifted LDE index, authenticated by a second Merkle path.

\paragraph{Range check AIR.} Value column $V$ plus $n$ bit columns
($1\le n\le24$), with challenges $\alpha_{\mathrm{decomp}}$,
$\{\alpha_j\}$, $\alpha_{\mathrm{bound}}$:
\[
C=\alpha_{\mathrm{decomp}}\frac{V-\sum_j2^jC_j}{Z_H}
+\sum_j\alpha_j\frac{C_j(C_j-1)}{Z_H}
+\alpha_{\mathrm{bound}}\frac{V-\mathrm{value}}{x-1}.
\]
Booleanity doubles the masked degree; masked callers should size blowup and
queries for a rate near $2/\beta$. The verifier caps the declared bound at
the largest honest masked degree.

\paragraph{Table membership AIR.}
\[
C(x)=\alpha_{\mathrm{member}}\frac{\prod_{t\in T}(v-t)}{Z_H(x)}
+\alpha_{\mathrm{bound}}\frac{v-\mathrm{value}}{x-1}.
\]
The honest composition bound is $|T|(n+k-1)-n$ for $n$ rows and $k$ maskers.
The tiny-table \texttt{for\_security} tuner floors blowup at 4 so FRI always
has a valid final layer.

\paragraph{Hash-folded IVC.} Segments of \texttt{seg\_steps} Fibonacci rows
are committed independently with explicit in/out claims. The verifier checks
chain links, then verifies one shared FRI proof over
$V=\sum_s\gamma_sC_s$ with per-step challenges. A far-from-code segment keeps
the combination far with overwhelming probability over the gammas. The
statement is existential over the number of segments; callers requiring an
exact step count must check \texttt{proof.steps.len()}. The accumulator
retains each segment's LDE, Merkle tree, and composition until finalization.

\paragraph{Register-machine execution.} A public two-register straight-line
program runs from a private initial state to a public output. Opcodes are
\texttt{Add}, \texttt{Mul}, \texttt{Swap}, \texttt{SetA}, \texttt{SetB}, and
\texttt{Halt}; the last row is the public output and has no outgoing
transition. Public one-hot selectors and immediate polynomials drive
transition numerators $T,U$:
\[
\begin{aligned}
C(x)={}&\alpha_0\frac{T(x)}{Z_{\mathrm{trans}}(x)}
+\alpha_1\frac{U(x)}{Z_{\mathrm{trans}}(x)}\\
&+\alpha_2\frac{A(x)-a_{\mathrm{last}}}{x-w_{\mathrm{last}}}
+\alpha_3\frac{B(x)-b_{\mathrm{last}}}{x-w_{\mathrm{last}}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Plain composition degree is below $2n-1$, so blowup is at least 8. Masked
columns have degree $\mathrm{padded}-1$; the \texttt{Mul} transition is
quadratic, so the prover declares $2(n+n_{\mathrm{rand}}-1)$ and the verifier
accepts up to $2\cdot\mathrm{padded}-2$. Each query reveals the current and
shifted rows, so the mask budget is $2Q$. The program and output remain
public; there is no control flow or memory.

\paragraph{Recursion fragment.} \texttt{recursion} proves one FRI query's fold
chain as a small STARK. Public inputs are the outer \texttt{lde\_len}, leaf,
per-depth challenges, the layer-0 quad, and the output; the witness is the
sequence of quads and folded outputs. Constraints cover the fold identity,
the selected next-layer element, the initial quad, and the final output. The
fragment does not yet verify BLAKE3 paths, the final degree check, or
transcript derivation inside the AIR.

\section{Zero-knowledge masking}

Masked proving maps a column $T$ of degree $<n$ to
\[
M(x)=T(x)+Z_H(x)R(x),
\]
where $R$ has $n_{\mathrm{rand}}$ uniform coefficients. $Z_H$ vanishes on
every base row, so trace values and AIR quotient identities are preserved.
Off-domain, the mask term is nonzero. Evaluations of $R$ at $k$ distinct
points are jointly uniform when $n_{\mathrm{rand}}\ge k$ (Vandermonde),
giving perfect hiding of query responses.

\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
\toprule
Protocol & Revealed values per column per query & Minimum $n_{\mathrm{rand}}$ \\
\midrule
\texttt{range}, \texttt{table} & one point & $Q$ \\
\texttt{vm} & current and shifted rows & $2Q$ \\
\texttt{air} & eight-row leaf and shifted leaf & $16Q$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Masking budgets for the values actually revealed.}
\end{table}

The composition degree grows accordingly; callers must size
\texttt{final\_size} for the masked LDE. Verification is unchanged, and
masked false statements still fail. This is query-response hiding, not full
program privacy: public inputs, Merkle roots, and composition commitments
remain visible. \texttt{lookup}, \texttt{sumcheck}, \texttt{upcs},
\texttt{gemini}, and \texttt{basefold} are not hiding.

default scalar path remains the supported stable build.

\section{Security evaluation}

\paragraph{Assumptions.} BLAKE3 collision/preimage resistance; FRI
soundness under the Johnson-radius heuristic; Fiat--Shamir in the (quantum)
random-oracle model; standard Reed--Solomon list-decoding bounds. No
discrete-log, pairing, or setup assumptions: the construction is Shor-inert
by design.

\paragraph{Enforced invariants.} Bilateral rate gate; verifier-derived
domains and query indices; non-vacuous final divided-difference check;
cap/path shape pins before hashing; direction bits bound to claimed indices;
\texttt{fri-dims} dimension binding; canonical-only decoding; PoW bound to
history with queries sampled after; committed read-checks in
\texttt{lookup\_pcs}; correct masked composition bounds in VM; and next-row
openings authenticated against the same trace cap.

\paragraph{Adversarial evidence.} The current tree passes 277 library tests,
13 security tests with 479 hostile attempts, 10 red-team tests with 37
targeted attacks plus a 500-input garbage storm, 11 hardening tests including
exhaustive per-bit AIR decoder fuzzing and 14,000 random mutations across
seven encodings, and 5 documentation tests: 316 tests total, plus the
coverage-guided \texttt{fuzz/decode} target. Every measured forgery is
rejected; hostile input fails closed with \texttt{Err}, never a panic.

\paragraph{Closed findings.} Internal adversarial review has closed Merkle
path index substitution across all verifiers and hand-rolled FRI/BaseFold
walks; one-point FRI final layers and fold-free FRI; final cap not bound to
\texttt{final\_poly}; \texttt{lookup\_pcs} read-checks using uncommitted
shifted MLEs; VM masked \texttt{Mul} degree under-declaration; AIR/VM ZK mask
budgets undercounting revealed values; batched BaseFold openings using the
equality table instead of the multilinear basis table; and prover allocation
ordering, cap-height/roundtrip limits, recursion domain bounds, checked
buffer arithmetic, and transcript/grinding hardening.

\paragraph{Residual risk.} The system is unaudited. The query/PoW figures are
conjectured. The small-table AIR scales with table size. \texttt{lookup\_pcs}
is not zero-knowledge and does not constrain columns to the base field.
BaseFold, Gemini, VM, and recursion are prototypes. Side channels in the host
OS, compiler, or CPU are out of scope. Do not deploy for high-value
statements before an external audit.

\section{Related work}

z0p follows the STARK/FRI lineage: Ben-Sasson et al.'s STARKs and FRI,
DEEP-FRI, and BCS-style Fiat--Shamir. Production systems such as Plonky2 and
RISC Zero share the BabyBear-plus-FRI engineering core but retain larger
dependency footprints. z0p differs by taking dependency-freedom and
auditable minimalism as hard constraints, and by publishing adversarial test
evidence alongside performance claims. Lookups follow the LogUp
logarithmic-derivative design. The hash is BLAKE3; Fiat--Shamir is classical.

\section{Roadmap and conclusion}

The evidence package is ready for external review. The next steps are a
hiding PCS and sumcheck for private tables; batched BaseFold openings to
replace the eight separate \texttt{lookup\_pcs} openings; self-proving
recursion with BLAKE3 path checks, final-degree checks, and transcript
derivation inside the AIR; control flow, memory, instruction privacy, and a
general circuit compiler for the zkVM direction; and broader SIMD coverage.

z0p demonstrates that a useful, transparent STARK stack can be implemented
from first principles in safe Rust with zero dependencies and a small,
auditable trusted base. The current artifact is deliberately positioned as
pre-audit research software: the engineering, adversarial evidence, formal
specification, and measured evaluation are complete enough for external
review, while the soundness figures remain conjectured and the prototype
components remain explicitly labeled.
