\begin{abstract}
We present \textbf{z0p}, a complete zero-knowledge proving stack implemented
from first principles in safe Rust with no dependencies beyond the compiler
and the operating system. Binary fields, the BabyBear prime field, a quartic
extension field, BLAKE3-256, Merkle trees, a parallel NTT, FRI, sumcheck,
univariate and multilinear polynomial commitments, LogUp lookups, four
STARK-style AIR systems, hash-folded IVC, a minimal register-machine AIR,
and zero-knowledge masking are owned by a single crate under
\texttt{\#![forbid(unsafe\_code)]}. The construction is transparent: no
trusted setup, no elliptic curves, no pairings. Security reduces to
BLAKE3-256, Reed--Solomon proximity testing, and Fiat--Shamir.

Production parameters target a \emph{conjectured} 100-bit
query/proof-of-work schedule: rate $1/16$, 40 queries, and 20-bit grinding.
Algebraic challenges are drawn from the quartic extension
$\zFpFour=\zFpTwo[v]/(v^2-u)$, $\zFpTwo=\zFp[u]/(u^2-11)$, giving approximately 124
bits of per-challenge algebraic entropy. On a 16-core reference machine,
release profile, \texttt{rustc}~1.99.0, a 1,048,576-step Fibonacci AIR
proves in 1.73355~s (1.653~$\mu$s/step, 604,872 steps/s), verifies in
1.13~ms, and produces a 203,553-byte (198.8~KiB) proof. Verification is
essentially independent of trace size: FRI domain points are derived on
demand rather than materialized.

The artifact is validated by 277 library tests, 13 security tests with 479
hostile attempts, 10 red-team tests with 37 targeted attacks plus a
500-input garbage storm, 11 hardening tests including exhaustive per-bit AIR
decoder fuzzing and 14,000 random mutations across seven encodings, and 5
documentation tests. Every measured forgery is rejected; hostile input fails
closed with \texttt{Err}, never a panic. The system is explicitly
\emph{unaudited}: the contribution is an auditable engineering artifact and a
reproducible evidence package, not a claim of production-grade end-to-end
soundness.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Practical zero-knowledge infrastructure asks its users to trust a large
surface: pairing-friendly curves whose security assumptions are contested,
multi-party setup ceremonies, deep dependency trees, and protocol behavior
described in papers but not reproducible from the code that runs. At the
same time, large-scale quantum computing threatens every deployed
pairing- and discrete-log-based proof system. The systems that survive that
transition will be hash-based, transparently set up, small enough to audit,
and tested hard enough to believe.

\textbf{Contributions.} z0p is, to our knowledge, one of the smallest
complete STARK stacks---fields, hash, Merkle trees, NTT, polynomial
commitments, AIR systems, lookups, and zero-knowledge masking---implemented
from scratch in safe Rust with zero runtime dependencies:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item A transparent, plausibly post-quantum design: BLAKE3-256 digests and
FRI Reed--Solomon commitments over BabyBear. No curves, no ceremonies.
\item Production parameter sizing in one call
(\texttt{Params::for\_security(\dots, 100)}): blowup 16, queries,
grinding, final layer, and cap height jointly optimized for a conjectured
100-bit query/PoW schedule. All algebraic challenges use a quartic extension
field.
\item Competitive scalar performance with parallelism over
\texttt{std::thread} only: 1M-step proofs at approximately 605k steps/s,
millisecond verification, and logarithmically growing proofs.
\item Adversarial validation as a first-class artifact: every forgery class
we could construct is tested continuously, including a fuzzer that once
caught a genuine proof-malleability hole (since closed by binding dimensions
into the transcript).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Status.} Version 0.3.0 is unaudited research software. The
approximately 100-bit figure is a query/proof-of-work schedule under a FRI
heuristic, not an end-to-end soundness theorem. The paper states exactly what
is enforced by code, what is conjectured, and what remains out of scope.

\subsection{Related work}

STARKs~\cite{BBHR18a} and the FRI proximity test~\cite{BBHR18b},
strengthened by DEEP-FRI~\cite{BBHR19} and compiled via BCS-style
Fiat--Shamir~\cite{BCS16}, are our direct lineage. Production STARK
toolchains (notably Plonky2/Polygon Zero~\cite{Pol22} and RISC Zero) share
the BabyBear-plus-FRI engineering core but retain larger dependency
footprints. We differ in taking dependency-freedom and auditable minimalism
as hard constraints, and in publishing adversarial test evidence alongside
performance claims. Our lookups follow the LogUp logarithmic-derivative
design~\cite{Hab22}. The hash is BLAKE3~\cite{BLAKE3}; Fiat--Shamir is
classical~\cite{FS86}.

\section{Design goals and threat model}

\paragraph{Goals.} (1) From zero: every cryptographic layer is implemented in
the crate; \texttt{cargo tree} is one node. (2) Transparent and plausibly
post-quantum: BLAKE3 and Reed--Solomon proximity testing; no curves, pairings,
or setup. (3) Safe by construction: \texttt{\#![forbid(unsafe\_code)]},
fallible public APIs, canonical fail-closed decoding, hostile parameters
re-validated at every entry point. (4) Quantified security: explicit query and
proof-of-work budgets. (5) Evidence over claims: adversarial suites,
byte-exact proof-size assertions, and a pinned benchmark regression gate.

\paragraph{Non-goals.} z0p is not an audited production system, a general
circuit compiler, a zkVM, a private-table lookup protocol, or a
side-channel-hardened hardware token. The \texttt{lookup} and
\texttt{sumcheck} APIs expose tables in clear; \texttt{lookup\_pcs} hides
columns from the wire but is not zero-knowledge; \texttt{basefold},
\texttt{gemini}, \texttt{lookup\_pcs}, \texttt{vm}, and \texttt{recursion}
are prototypes.

\paragraph{Threat model.} The verifier is the adversary-facing surface.
Untrusted inputs are proof and commitment bytes, public parameters
(including hand-built \texttt{Params} structs whose fields are public), and
public statements. The verifier must fail closed with \texttt{Err}, never
panic, and never perform unbounded allocation or work. It must derive all
challenge-dependent data from the transcript and bind every public input
before sampling challenges.

\paragraph{Trust base.} The Rust compiler and standard library; BLAKE3-256
as a collision-resistant hash; the Reed--Solomon/FRI proximity heuristic and
Fiat--Shamir in the (quantum) random-oracle model; the correctness of the
field and NTT implementations, cross-tested against independent
Horner/\texttt{\%} references. No trusted setup, elliptic-curve group,
pairing, or discrete-log assumption is used.

\section{Preliminaries}

\paragraph{Fields.} Program words use binary fields $GF(2^8)$,
$GF(2^{64})$, $GF(2^{128})$. Polynomial domains use BabyBear
\[
p = 2^{31}-2^{27}+1 = 2013265921,\qquad p-1 = 2^{27}\cdot 15,
\]
with generator $31$. Multiplicative subgroups of order $2^k$ exist for
$k\le 27$. Elements serialize as four canonical little-endian bytes;
multiplication uses Barrett reduction with
$\mu=\lfloor 2^{64}/p\rfloor$; inversion is Fermat
($a^{p-2}$, mapping zero to zero); batch inversion uses Montgomery's trick.

All algebraic challenges use the tower
\[
\zFpTwo=\zFp[u]/(u^2-11),\qquad
\zFpFour=\zFpTwo[v]/(v^2-u),
\]
represented as $a+bu+cv+duv$ with four canonical coordinates. $11$ is a
quadratic non-residue in $\zFp$, and $u$ is a non-square in $\zFpTwo$. Inversion
uses the tower norm: for $x=c+dv$,
$x^{-1}=(c-dv)/(c^2-ud^2)$, so one $\zFpTwo$ inversion suffices.

\paragraph{Domains and NTT.} The trace domain is
$H=\{1,\omega,\dots,\omega^{N-1}\}$; the evaluation domain is the coset
$D=\gamma\cdot\langle\Omega\rangle$ of size $M=N\beta$, with coset shift
$\gamma=31$ and $M\le 2^{27}$. The vanishing polynomial is
$Z_H(x)=x^N-1$. The NTT is a table-driven Cooley--Tukey transform with
bit-reversal, deterministic parallel stages, and the inverse scaling folded
into the last stage. Reed--Solomon extension interpolates and evaluates on
the coset.

\paragraph{BLAKE3 and Merkle trees.} Unkeyed BLAKE3-256 is implemented from
the specification: 7-round ARX compression, the standard message
permutation, flags \texttt{CHUNK\_START}, \texttt{CHUNK\_END},
\texttt{PARENT}, \texttt{ROOT}, 1024-byte chunks, and Bao-style
merge-while-even stacking. Column trees use
\[
\mathrm{leaf}(d)=H(0x00\|d),\qquad
\mathrm{node}(l,r)=H(0x01\|l\|r).
\]
FRI layers commit one 64-byte leaf per quad (four 16-byte $\zFpFour$ values);
AIR trace layers commit one 64-byte leaf per eight joint rows. Caps expose an
upper tree layer of $2^h$ digests, and every path direction bit is bound to
the claimed leaf index. Cap height is capped at 8.

\paragraph{Transcript.} The transcript is append-only and length-prefix
framed. Each absorb writes
$(\text{label\_len},\text{label},\text{data\_len},\text{data})$ into a
streaming BLAKE3 state. A challenge clones that state, appends
$(\text{label\_len},\text{label},\text{counter})$, finalizes, and feeds the
output back. This is byte-identical to hashing the full concatenation but
independent of history length. $\zFp$ challenges reduce a 64-bit word modulo
$p$; query indices mask low bits for power-of-two domains; $\zFpFour$ challenges
reduce four coordinates.

\paragraph{Proof of work.} \texttt{grind(label,d)} finds the smallest nonce
with at least $d$ leading zero bits under the history commitment, using a
banded parallel search identical to a sequential scan. Protocol parameters
cap grinding at 32 bits; \texttt{grind} fails closed above 32.
\texttt{verify\_grind} checks the nonce and absorbs it only on success.
Queries are sampled after the nonce is absorbed.

\section{FRI polynomial commitment}

\subsection{Parameters and commitment}

A FRI parameter set is
\[
(\beta,Q,F,g,h),\qquad
\beta\ge 2,\quad Q\ge 1,\quad 2\le F\le 1024,\quad g\le 32,\quad h\le 8,
\]
with $F<M$ (at least one fold) and $M/F$ a power of four. \texttt{commit}
maps $n$ coefficients to $M=n\beta$ coset evaluations with a shifted NTT.
\texttt{commit\_evals} accepts already-expanded LDE values with a declared
degree bound $d<M$. Both paths validate parameters before the encoding NTT,
reject empty or non-power-of-two lengths, enforce $M\le 2^{27}$, and bind
$(M,n,d)$ under \texttt{fri-dims} before any challenge. The rate gate
\[
d+1\le M/2
\]
is enforced at prove and verify.

\subsection{Arity-4 folding}

Layers are bit-reversed. Each adjacent quad sits at
$(x,-x,j_0x,-j_0x)$ for the fixed fourth root $j_0$, sharing $x^4$. Writing
$P(X)=A+BX+CX^2+DX^3$ in $Y=X^4$, the fold is
\[
\zfold(e_0,e_1,e_2,e_3)=A+r_0B+r_1C+r_2D,
\]
with
\[
\begin{aligned}
A&=(e_0+e_1+e_2+e_3)/4,\\
B&=((e_0-e_1)-j_0(e_2-e_3))/(4x),\\
C&=((e_0+e_1)-(e_2+e_3))/(4x^2),\\
D&=((e_0-e_1)+j_0(e_2-e_3))/(4x^3),
\end{aligned}
\]
and $(r_0,r_1,r_2)\in\zFpFour^3$. Point inverses are base-field values and are
batch-inverted once per layer. Each fold quarters the degree.

\subsection{Proof and verification}

A proof carries the final polynomial in the clear, one opening per query
(four $\zFpFour$ values per folded quad plus the middle Merkle path segment), and
a PoW nonce. The verifier:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.3em]
\item validates dimensions, cap shapes, fold count, and the rate gate;
\item absorbs dimensions, cap digests, and the final polynomial;
\item checks the PoW nonce against the replayed history;
\item verifies the final layer by divided differences against residual
$d\gg 2f$ at the true folded domain points;
\item recomputes the final layer cap from \texttt{final\_poly} and compares
it to the commitment;
\item samples query indices after grinding and checks every Merkle path and
fold relation, chaining intermediate parents forward and the last parent
into \texttt{final\_poly}.
\end{enumerate}

The final degree check is non-vacuous: a one-point final layer would let any
value interpolate a degree-0 polynomial, so $F\ge 2$ is mandatory and
\[
(d\gg 2f)+1<F
\]
is enforced. A fold-free instance ($F=M$) is rejected because it has no query
openings binding \texttt{final\_poly} to the commitment.

\subsection{Conjectured soundness}

Under the Johnson-radius heuristic, a word at distance $\delta$ from the code
survives one query with probability at most $1-\delta$, where
$\delta\approx 1-\sqrt{\rho}$.

\section{Sumcheck and multilinear polynomials}

A multilinear polynomial with $n$ variables stores $2^n$ evaluations in
MSB-first order. \texttt{evaluate} folds the least-significant variable
first. The equality polynomial is
\[
\zeq(r,x)=\prod_i\bigl(r_ix_i+(1-r_i)(1-x_i)\bigr).
\]
A summand is a sum of terms
$f(x)=\sum_k c_k\prod_{j\in\mathrm{factors}_k}\mathrm{mle}_j(x)$ with
$c_k\in\zFpFour$. Sumcheck proves $S=\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^n}f(x)$ by sending round
polynomials of degree at most the maximum factor count. Verification checks
$g_i(0)+g_i(1)=g_{i-1}(r_{i-1})$, samples $r_i\in\zFpFour$, and finally checks
the oracle value. If the oracle is committed, each final value is opened at
the challenge point.

\section{Univariate, Gemini, and BaseFold commitments}

\subsection{UPCs}

\texttt{upcs} commits a univariate polynomial as a Merkle-committed
Reed--Solomon LDE. To prove $g(\beta)=y$, the prover commits
$q=(g-y)/(X-\beta)$ and proves both $g$ and $q$ low-degree with FRI; the
verifier checks $(X-\beta)q=g-y$ at the quotient's query points. Multi-point
openings use $I$ interpolating the claims and $Z=\prod_j(X-p_j)$, with
$q=(g-I)/Z$ and $g=I+Zq$. When the points determine the polynomial
($k=\mathrm{poly\_len}$), the quotient is zero and the verifier checks $g$
against the interpolant directly.

\subsection{Gemini}

Gemini commits the monomial-coefficient encoding
$g_0(X)=\sum_b c_bX^{\mathrm{index}(b)}$ and proves $f(r)=y$ by folding one
variable at a time:
\[
g_{i-1}(X)=A_i(X^2)+XB_i(X^2),\qquad
g_i(Y)=A_i(Y)+r_iB_i(Y).
\]
Each committed layer is opened once with a multi-point UPCs proof at all
points its relations use. Layer commitments are absorbed before the fold
betas. Proof size is linear, not constant. Each fold identity has degree
below $2^{n-i+1}$, so one random check misses a nonzero identity with
probability at most $2^{n-i+1}/p^4$.

\subsection{BaseFold prototype}

BaseFold commits the coefficient encoding with FRI and reduces $f(r)=y$ to a
coefficient-oracle evaluation $c(\rho)$ with sumcheck. The prover folds the
LDE with the Möbius/eq rule
\[
(1-\rho_i)E(X)+\rho_iO(X)
\]
rather than the coefficient-encoding rule $E(X)+\rho_iO(X)$, so the final
FRI constant is exactly $c(\rho)$. The verifier recomputes
\[
w(\rho)=\prod_i(1-\rho_i+\rho_i r_i)
\]
and checks $f(r)=c(\rho)w(\rho)$. Proofs carry one root, $n-1$ layer roots,
sumcheck rounds, and one pair plus one Merkle path per layer per query.
Optional caps store a digest frontier per layer and hash it back to the layer
root. Batched multi-point openings use the multilinear basis table and are
capped at 1024 points. The prototype is not wired into the AIR/STARK stack
and has no end-to-end soundness proof; it backs the committed lookup.

\section{Lookup arguments}

\subsection{LogUp in clear}

For witness $w$ and table $t$ padded to powers of two, the prover computes
first-index multiplicities $m$, samples $\beta$ avoiding poles, and forms
\[
q^w_i=\frac{1}{\beta+w_i},\qquad
q^t_j=\frac{m_j}{\beta+t_j}.
\]
Four sumchecks establish the rational identity
$\sum_i q^w_i=\sum_j q^t_j$ and the read-checks
$q^w_i(\beta+w_i)=1$, $q^t_j(\beta+t_j)=m_j$. The verifier has witness and
table in clear and recomputes canonical multiplicities exactly; fractional
multiplicities are rejected. The API is sound for membership but not hiding.

\subsection{Committed LogUp}

\texttt{lookup\_pcs} commits witness, table, multiplicities, and both quotient
columns with BaseFold. The verifier sees five commitments and eight openings.
The read-checks use the committed columns directly:
\[
\zeq\cdot\bigl(q\cdot(\beta+v)-\mathrm{target}\bigr)=0,
\]
expanded as $\zeq qv+\beta\zeq q-\zeq\,\mathrm{target}$. A fresh $\gamma$ folds
the total multiplicity weight into the sum-t claim:
\[
\sum(q^t+\gamma m)=\sum q^w+\gamma W.
\]
The verifier rejects $\gamma=0$. Every final sumcheck value is opened against
its commitment. At 1,024-in-1,024 with $B16Q40G20C4$, the prototype measures
273.01~ms prove, 5.77~ms verify, and 642,443 bytes. It is committed-input but
not zero-knowledge and does not constrain columns to the base field.

\section{AIR systems}

\subsection{Fibonacci AIR}

Trace $A[0]=a_0$, $B[0]=b_0$, $A[i+1]=B[i]$, $B[i+1]=A[i]+B[i]$, claim
$B[N-1]=b_{\mathrm{last}}$. With $w_{\mathrm{last}}=\omega^{N-1}$ and
$Z_{\mathrm{trans}}(x)=(x^N-1)/(x-w_{\mathrm{last}})$:
\[
\begin{aligned}
C(x)={}&\alpha_0\frac{a'-b}{Z_{\mathrm{trans}}(x)}
+\alpha_1\frac{b'-(a+b)}{Z_{\mathrm{trans}}(x)}\\
&+\alpha_2\frac{a-a_0}{x-1}
+\alpha_3\frac{b-b_0}{x-1}
+\alpha_4\frac{b-b_{\mathrm{last}}}{x-w_{\mathrm{last}}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Five \texttt{fib/alpha} challenges; $\deg C<N$ iff the trace is valid.
Dividing, not multiplying, by $Z_{\mathrm{trans}}$ is load-bearing. Blowup is
at least 4. Next-row values are the same committed columns at the
$\omega$-shifted LDE index, authenticated by a second Merkle path.

\subsection{Range check AIR}

Value column $V$ plus $n$ bit columns ($1\le n\le24$), with challenges
$\alpha_{\mathrm{decomp}}$, $\{\alpha_j\}$, $\alpha_{\mathrm{bound}}$:
\[
C=\alpha_{\mathrm{decomp}}\frac{V-\sum_j2^jC_j}{Z_H}
+\sum_j\alpha_j\frac{C_j(C_j-1)}{Z_H}
+\alpha_{\mathrm{bound}}\frac{V-\mathrm{value}}{x-1}.
\]
Booleanity doubles the masked degree; masked callers should size blowup and
queries for a rate near $2/\beta$. The verifier caps the declared bound at
the largest honest masked degree.

\subsection{Table membership AIR}

\[
C(x)=\alpha_{\mathrm{member}}\frac{\prod_{t\in T}(v-t)}{Z_H(x)}
+\alpha_{\mathrm{bound}}\frac{v-\mathrm{value}}{x-1}.
\]
The honest composition bound is
$|T|(n+k-1)-n$ for $n$ rows and $k$ maskers. The tiny-table
\texttt{for\_security} tuner floors blowup at 4 so FRI always has a valid
final layer.

\subsection{Hash-folded IVC}

Segments of \texttt{seg\_steps} Fibonacci rows are committed independently
with explicit in/out claims. The verifier checks chain links, then verifies
one shared FRI proof over
\[
V=\sum_s\gamma_sC_s
\]
with per-step challenges. A far-from-code segment keeps the combination far
with overwhelming probability over the gammas. The statement is existential
over the number of segments; callers requiring an exact step count must check
\texttt{proof.steps.len()}. The accumulator retains each segment's LDE,
Merkle tree, and composition until finalization.

\subsection{Register-machine execution}

A public two-register straight-line program runs from a private initial state
to a public output. Opcodes are \texttt{Add}, \texttt{Mul}, \texttt{Swap},
\texttt{SetA}, \texttt{SetB}, and \texttt{Halt}; the last row is the public
output and has no outgoing transition. Public one-hot selectors and immediate
polynomials drive transition numerators $T,U$:
\[
\begin{aligned}
C(x)={}&\alpha_0\frac{T(x)}{Z_{\mathrm{trans}}(x)}
+\alpha_1\frac{U(x)}{Z_{\mathrm{trans}}(x)}\\
&+\alpha_2\frac{A(x)-a_{\mathrm{last}}}{x-w_{\mathrm{last}}}
+\alpha_3\frac{B(x)-b_{\mathrm{last}}}{x-w_{\mathrm{last}}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Plain composition degree is below $2n-1$, so blowup is at least 8. Masked
columns have degree $\mathrm{padded}-1$; the \texttt{Mul} transition is
quadratic, so the prover declares $2(n+n_{\mathrm{rand}}-1)$ and the verifier
accepts up to $2\cdot\mathrm{padded}-2$. Each query reveals the current and
shifted rows, so the mask budget is $2Q$. The program and output remain
public; there is no control flow or memory.

\subsection{Recursion fragment}

\texttt{recursion} proves one FRI query's fold chain as a small STARK. Public
inputs are the outer \texttt{lde\_len}, leaf, per-depth challenges, the
layer-0 quad, and the output; the witness is the sequence of quads and folded
outputs. Constraints cover the fold identity, the selected next-layer
element, the initial quad, and the final output. The fragment does not yet
verify BLAKE3 paths, the final degree check, or transcript derivation inside
the AIR.

\section{Zero-knowledge masking}

Masked proving maps a column $T$ of degree $<n$ to
\[
M(x)=T(x)+Z_H(x)R(x),
\]
where $R$ has $n_{\mathrm{rand}}$ uniform coefficients. $Z_H$ vanishes on
every base row, so trace values and AIR quotient identities are preserved.
Off-domain, the mask term is nonzero. Evaluations of $R$ at $k$ distinct
points are jointly uniform when $n_{\mathrm{rand}}\ge k$
(Vandermonde), giving perfect hiding of query responses.

\section{Security analysis}\label{sec:security}

\paragraph{Assumptions.} BLAKE3 collision/preimage resistance; FRI
soundness under the Johnson-radius heuristic; Fiat--Shamir in the (quantum)
random-oracle model; standard Reed--Solomon list-decoding bounds. No
discrete-log, pairing, or setup assumptions: the construction is Shor-inert
by design.

\paragraph{Enforced invariants.} Bilateral rate gate; verifier-derived
domains and query indices; non-vacuous final divided-difference check;
cap/path shape pins before hashing; direction bits bound to claimed indices;
\texttt{fri-dims} dimension binding; canonical-only decoding; PoW bound to
history with queries sampled after; committed read-checks in
\texttt{lookup\_pcs}; correct masked composition bounds in VM; and
next-row openings authenticated against the same trace cap.

\paragraph{Closed findings.} Internal adversarial review has closed, among
others: Merkle path index substitution across all verifiers and hand-rolled
FRI/BaseFold walks; one-point FRI final layers and fold-free FRI; final cap
not bound to \texttt{final\_poly}; \texttt{lookup\_pcs} read-checks using
uncommitted shifted MLEs; VM masked \texttt{Mul} degree under-declaration;
AIR/VM ZK mask budgets undercounting revealed values; batched BaseFold
openings using the equality table instead of the multilinear basis table; and
prover allocation ordering, cap-height/roundtrip limits, recursion domain
bounds, checked buffer arithmetic, and transcript/grinding hardening.
Regression tests and reproducer descriptions are in
\texttt{docs/security-tests.md}.

\paragraph{Residual risk.} The system is unaudited. The query/PoW figures are
conjectured. The small-table AIR scales with table size.
\texttt{lookup\_pcs} is not zero-knowledge and does not constrain columns to
the base field. BaseFold, Gemini, VM, and recursion are prototypes. Side
channels in the host OS, compiler, or CPU are out of scope. Do not deploy for
high-value statements before an external audit.

\subsection{FRI query layout}

For layer $d$, a query opens the four values of the quad containing the
current index and a Merkle path from the quad's parent to the layer root or
selected cap entry. The verifier recomputes the leaf digest from the four
values, checks the path and direction bits, folds the quad with the layer
challenge, and requires the parent to be the selected value in the next
layer. The last parent must equal the corresponding element of
\texttt{final\_poly}. The final layer cap is recomputed from
\texttt{final\_poly}, so the commitment has no unconstrained digest.

\subsection{AIR opening layout}

An AIR opening contains the natural-order LDE index, the column values at
that index, the same columns at the $\omega$-shifted index, two Merkle paths,
and (for the Fibonacci AIR) two 64-byte leaves containing eight joint rows
each. The verifier checks the row at \texttt{(index \& 7) * 8}, authenticates
the leaf at \texttt{index / 8}, recomputes the shifted index, authenticates
the second leaf, and evaluates the composition identity at the on-demand
domain point. Range/table/VM/fold/recursion openings use analogous row
layouts without the eight-row packing.

\section{Formal security statements}

\paragraph{Assumptions.} We state the following conditionally on the
standard FRI heuristic and the random-oracle model; they are not
unconditional theorems.

\begin{definition}[Rate gate]
A FRI instance with declared degree bound $d$ and LDE length $M$ satisfies
the rate gate if $d+1\le M/2$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Non-vacuous final check]
If $F\ge2$ and $(d\gg 2f)+1<F$, the final divided-difference check is not
vacuous: there exist $F$ points and values for which a polynomial of degree
greater than the residual bound is rejected.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Sketch]
With $F$ distinct points, the interpolation space has dimension $F$. If the
residual bound were at least $F-1$, every value vector would admit a
degree-$\le F-1$ interpolant. Requiring the bound below $F-1$ leaves a
nonempty set of divided differences that must vanish. The implementation
checks exactly those differences.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Merkle index binding]
For a path of length $k$ and claimed index $i$, verification succeeds only if
the direction bits equal the low $k$ bits of $i$ and the recomputed root
matches. Consequently a valid opening for leaf $j$ cannot be accepted at an
index $i\ne j$ with the same path length.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Sketch]
The verifier iterates over the path and rejects the first direction bit that
disagrees with the corresponding index bit. If all bits agree, the recomputed
position is $i$; a different leaf $j$ would require a collision in the hash
chain or a different direction sequence.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Committed read-check]
In \texttt{lookup\_pcs}, the read-check summand uses the committed witness,
table, and multiplicity columns as MLE factors. A prover that changes the
witness/table after the commitment must either open the commitment at the
sumcheck point to a different value or produce a sumcheck final value that
does not satisfy the committed polynomial; both are rejected by the opening
and final-value checks.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Mask hiding, conditional]
If the number of distinct masked evaluations revealed per column is at most
$n_{\mathrm{rand}}$, then the mask evaluations are jointly uniform over the
revealed points by Vandermonde surjectivity, so the revealed values are
independent of the witness.
\end{lemma}

\begin{conjecture}[Query/PoW schedule]
Under the Johnson-radius heuristic, rate $1/16$, 40 queries, and 20-bit
grinding give approximately 100 bits of query/PoW security; rate $1/8$ with
12 queries gives approximately 18 bits. These figures exclude the
finite-field algebraic term and are not an end-to-end theorem.
\end{conjecture}

\section{Implementation and audit map}

\paragraph{Concurrency.} NTT stages at least $2^{16}$, Merkle levels, FRI
folding, composition, and PoW search use \texttt{std::thread::scope}.
Chunk boundaries depend only on lengths; every output is a pure function of
its inputs. Tests pin parallel-vs-sequential identity at $2^{16}$ and
$2^{17}$ NTT sizes and for Merkle batch hashing.

\paragraph{Memory discipline.} The verifier derives FRI domain points on
demand and never builds the full layer stack. Counts are capped before
allocation. Prover trace/composition buffers use checked multiplication.
The FRI final layer is capped at 1024; Merkle caps at 256 digests. The fold
accumulator retains per-segment state until finalization and documents its
$O(\text{segments}\times\text{LDE})$ prover memory.

\paragraph{Audit map.} Reviewers should start with \texttt{codec},
\texttt{transcript}, \texttt{merkle}, and \texttt{fri}; then fields
(\texttt{babybear}, \texttt{ext4}, \texttt{gf2}); then the polynomial core
(\texttt{ntt}, \texttt{mle}, \texttt{sumcheck}); then statements
(\texttt{air}, \texttt{range}, \texttt{table}, \texttt{fold},
\texttt{vm}, \texttt{recursion}); then masking and the experimental
commitments/lookups. \texttt{tests/security.rs},
\texttt{tests/redteam.rs}, and \texttt{tests/hardening.rs} map each invariant
to a regression.

\end{center}
